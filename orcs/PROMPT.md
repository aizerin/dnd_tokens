# Orc Shaman Artwork Prompt

The Orc Raider, Archer, and Chief come from the previously generated set. The Orc Shaman was newly created with the built-in image generator. The existing orcs were used only as references for style and faction consistency.

> Use case: logo-brand. Asset type: source artwork for a flush two-colour 25 mm FDM-printed tabletop token. Input images: Images 1, 2, and 3 are style and creature-family references only. Create a completely new original Orc Shaman glyph that clearly belongs to the same orc faction. Do not copy a reference pose or its equipment. Primary request: exactly one centered black-and-white pictogram of an Orc Shaman, shown as a compact front-facing head-and-shoulders emblem. Give him the same broad orc jaw, heavy brow, large lower tusks, and muscular shoulders as the faction references. Distinguish the shaman with a wide primitive ritual bone headdress featuring one large simplified skull above the brow and one thick crooked ceremonial staff fused tightly alongside the shoulder. The skull headdress and staff must be visually dominant enough to distinguish him from the raider, archer, and chief. No helmet, no axe, no arrow. Print constraints: final artwork approximately 18 mm wide on a 25 mm token, printed with a 0.4 mm FDM nozzle. There will be no later dilation, erosion, stroke thickening, or cleanup. Every black stroke, connector, facial feature, and important white gap must be broad, approximately 5% of the artwork width or more. Use thick rounded graphic shapes, large clear cutouts, generous padding, and a visually connected compact composition. Keep the headdress and staff connected with stout bridges. Color and background: pure solid black artwork on a pure white background, crisp hard edges. Avoid: enclosing circle, token rim, border, text, letters, numbers, gray, colour, gradients, shadows, texture, perspective, 3D rendering, fine linework, thin bones, tiny holes, isolated floating fragments, feathers, extra objects, and extra weapons.

The technical conversion only traces the artwork boundary. It does not use dilation, erosion, or artwork thickening.
